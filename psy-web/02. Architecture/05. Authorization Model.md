
**System:** PsyWeb Backend  
**Security Model:** RBAC + Account Status + Domain-Level Access Rules

---

# Overview

The system uses **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** combined with account status, domain-specific state, ownership rules, and business constraints.

Authentication determines who the user is.

Authorization determines whether the authenticated user is allowed to perform a specific operation.

Authorization decisions may depend on:

- UserRole
- UserStatus
- SpecialistStatus, when specialist functionality is involved
- ownership of the requested resource
- current business state of the resource

Authorization is enforced at multiple levels:

- Spring Security and method-level access control
- service-level business authorization
- domain rules and state invariants

---

# 1. Roles

UserRole describes the primary platform role of a User account.

The current roles are:

- CLIENT
- SPECIALIST
- ADMIN

Role alone does not guarantee access to a business operation.

---

## CLIENT

### Description

A platform user who searches for specialists and uses consultation-related functionality.

### Typical Capabilities

A CLIENT may:

- search and view available specialists
- create reservations
- confirm their own reservations
- view their own bookings
- cancel their own reservations or bookings when business rules allow it
- leave reviews when eligible
- use communication features if enabled
- report the outcome of their own completed consultation

Client-specific operations require:

- UserRole.CLIENT
- UserStatus.ACTIVE

---

## SPECIALIST

### Description

A platform user who provides professional services through the platform.

### Typical Capabilities

A SPECIALIST may:

- manage specialist-specific profile data
- manage their availability
- view bookings related to them
- participate in consultation-related workflows
- use communication features if enabled
- report the outcome of consultations related to them

Access to specialist business functionality requires:

- UserRole.SPECIALIST
- UserStatus.ACTIVE
- SpecialistStatus.APPROVED

A SPECIALIST account may exist before approval, but specialist business capabilities remain restricted until approval is granted.

---

## ADMIN

### Description

A platform user responsible for administrative and moderation workflows.

### Typical Capabilities

An ADMIN may:

- review specialist applications
- approve or reject specialists
- moderate users
- access administrative data and operations
- perform system-management workflows

Administrative access requires:

- UserRole.ADMIN
- UserStatus.ACTIVE

ADMIN does not automatically bypass all domain rules or business invariants.

---

# 2. Account and Specialist State

The system uses separate state models for account access and specialist approval.

These states must not be merged into a single generic status model.

---

## 2.1 UserStatus

UserStatus belongs to the User domain and describes whether the account itself may use the platform.

Current values:

### ACTIVE

The account is enabled.

An ACTIVE account may access functionality allowed by its role and other applicable authorization rules.

### BLOCKED

The account is disabled from protected platform functionality.

A BLOCKED account must not be allowed to perform authenticated business operations.

---

## 2.2 SpecialistStatus

SpecialistStatus belongs to the Specialist domain and describes the professional approval state of a specialist.

Current values:

### PENDING

The specialist exists but has not yet been approved.

The user account may remain ACTIVE, but specialist business functionality is restricted.

### APPROVED

The specialist has passed moderation and may use specialist functionality, provided the linked User account is ACTIVE.

### REJECTED

The specialist has not passed moderation.

The linked User account and Specialist approval state remain separate concerns.

A REJECTED specialist does not receive APPROVED specialist capabilities.

The possibility of resubmitting a rejected specialist for approval is a separate business workflow and is not defined by this authorization model.

# 4. Authorization Rules

## AR-001 — Authentication Required for Protected Operations

Protected business operations require an authenticated user.

Public endpoints may be explicitly exempt from this rule.

---

## AR-002 — Role Check

An operation may require a specific UserRole.

Examples:

- reservation creation requires CLIENT
- availability management requires SPECIALIST
- specialist approval requires ADMIN

---

## AR-003 — Account Status Check

Protected business operations require UserStatus.ACTIVE.

A BLOCKED user must not be allowed to perform protected business operations even if their role would otherwise permit them.

---

## AR-004 — Specialist Approval Check

Specialist-specific business operations require:

- UserRole.SPECIALIST
- UserStatus.ACTIVE
- SpecialistStatus.APPROVED

PENDING or REJECTED specialists must not perform operations reserved for approved specialists.

---

## AR-005 — Ownership Rule

Users may access or modify only resources they own or participate in unless another rule explicitly grants broader access.

Examples:

- a client may cancel only their own reservation
- a client may confirm only their own reservation
- a client may access only their own client bookings
- a specialist may manage only their own availability
- a specialist may access only bookings related to them

---

## AR-006 — Business-State Rule

Authorization does not replace business-state validation.

A user may have permission to attempt an operation while the operation itself is still invalid because of resource state.

Examples:

- a CLIENT may be allowed to confirm reservations, but only an ACTIVE and unexpired reservation can actually be confirmed
- a SPECIALIST may manage availability, but a BOOKED slot cannot necessarily be modified
- a CLIENT may cancel a booking only when the booking state and cancellation policy allow it

Post-session outcome reporting is allowed only for a Booking that has reached the appropriate stage of its lifecycle for outcome reporting.

Normal Booking cancellation is handled by the cancellation flow and must not be duplicated through post-session outcome reporting.

---

## AR-007 — Administrative Access

ADMIN may perform explicitly defined administrative operations across the system.

ADMIN access does not imply unrestricted modification of every domain entity.

Administrative workflows should use the owning module's service-level API and must preserve domain invariants.

---

## AR-008 — Module Ownership

Authorization logic must not bypass module ownership.

For example:

Admin
→ SpecialistService
→ Specialist approval transition

is valid.

Direct modification of Specialist persistence from the Admin module is not.

---

# 5. Method-Level Security

Spring Security may be used for coarse-grained access control such as authentication and role checks.

Examples:

@PreAuthorize("hasRole('CLIENT')")

@PreAuthorize("hasRole('SPECIALIST')")

@PreAuthorize("hasRole('ADMIN')")

Method-level security is suitable for rules that can be evaluated from the authenticated principal and request context.

It should not be used as a replacement for domain-level business validation.

Resource ownership and business-state checks may remain in the service layer when they require loading domain data.

Resource ownership and session participation must be validated for post-session outcome reporting.

A CLIENT may report an outcome only for a Booking in which they are the client.

An APPROVED SPECIALIST may report an outcome only for a Booking related to that specialist.

Post-session outcome reporting is therefore subject to:

- authentication
- appropriate role
- active account status
- specialist approval, when applicable
- participation in the Booking
- applicable Booking state

---

# 6. Business-Level Authorization

Authorization rules that depend on persisted domain data are enforced in the service layer.

Examples include:

- reservation ownership validation
- booking ownership validation
- specialist approval validation
- specialist account eligibility
- slot ownership validation
- review eligibility
- cancellation permissions
- resource participation checks

The service layer is responsible for combining authenticated-user identity with domain state where required.

---

# 7. Combined Authorization Model

An access decision may be based on:

Authentication
+
UserRole
+
UserStatus
+
SpecialistStatus, when applicable
+
Ownership / Participation
+
Business State

These concerns answer different questions:

Authentication
→ Who is making the request?

UserRole
→ What type of platform actor is this?

UserStatus
→ Is the account enabled?

SpecialistStatus
→ Is the specialist approved to provide professional functionality?

Ownership / Participation
→ Is this user allowed to access this specific resource?

Business State
→ Is the requested operation valid for the resource right now?

A successful authorization decision does not guarantee that the business operation itself will succeed.

---

# 8. Denial Cases

## 401 Unauthorized

Used when the request cannot be associated with a valid authenticated principal.

Examples:

- missing authentication token on a protected endpoint
- invalid token
- expired token

---

## 403 Forbidden

Used when the caller is authenticated but is not permitted to perform the requested operation.

Examples:

- wrong role
- blocked account
- specialist is not approved
- ownership violation
- insufficient administrative permission

---

## Business Conflict / Invalid State

Some rejected operations are not authorization failures.

Examples:

- reservation already expired
- slot already reserved
- booking already cancelled
- cancellation is no longer allowed by business policy

These cases should be represented by appropriate business/API errors rather than automatically mapped to 403.

---

# 9. Future Extensions

The authorization model may later be extended with:

- more fine-grained permissions if role-based rules become insufficient
- dedicated policy components for complex resource access
- audit logging for sensitive administrative operations

Additional authorization mechanisms should only be introduced when concrete business requirements justify them.s

---

# Notes

Authorization in PsyWeb is intentionally split across different concerns rather than represented by a single status or permission flag.

The core distinctions are:

- UserRole defines the account's platform role
- UserStatus controls whether the account is enabled
- SpecialistStatus controls professional approval
- ownership limits access to individual resources
- business state determines whether an otherwise permitted action is currently valid

Security rules must preserve domain ownership and must not bypass business invariants.
